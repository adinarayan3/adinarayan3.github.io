`Abstract:`
A defining feature of episodic memory is that it can form after only a single encounter with something new. Recent neuroimaging work has shown that early visual brain regions (V1-V3) support surprisingly precise spatial memory recognition after just one viewing (Woodry et al., 2025). Separately, a large body of research shows that emotionally charged experiences tend to be remembered more durably than neutral ones. What remains unclear is whether the emotional charge of an image influences the precision of spatial memory after only a single exposure. Eight adults viewed a series of emotionally negative and neutral images presented once in one of four parafoveal locations. Participants then performed object recognition (old/new) and location recall tasks in which images were shown foveally. Our findings suggest that the brain’s spatial memory system operates largely independent of emotional content, and emotional arousal might prioritize a general sense of familiarity over the encoding of specific spatial information.

`Keywords:` one-shot memory; amygdala; visual cortex; emotional valence
